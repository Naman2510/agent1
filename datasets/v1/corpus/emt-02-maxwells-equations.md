# Unit 5: Electromagnetic Field Theory

## Chapter 12: Maxwell's Equations

### 12.1 The four equations

Maxwell's equations are four equations that together describe how electric and magnetic fields are
generated by charges, currents, and by each other.

1. **Gauss's law for electricity**: the electric flux through a closed surface is proportional to
   the enclosed charge.
2. **Gauss's law for magnetism**: the magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero — there are
   no magnetic monopoles.
3. **Faraday's law of induction**: a changing magnetic field induces an electric field (and hence
   an EMF in a loop).
4. **Ampère's law, with Maxwell's correction**: a magnetic field is produced both by electric
   current and by a changing electric field.

### 12.2 Displacement current

The fourth equation is the one students find hardest, because of the term Maxwell added to
Ampère's original law: the **displacement current**. Ampère's law alone predicted that a magnetic
field circulates around any current — but this failed for a charging capacitor, where current
flows in the wires on either side of the gap but not through the gap itself, and yet a magnetic
field is still observed around the gap.

Maxwell resolved this by proposing that a *changing electric field* between the capacitor plates
acts exactly like a current for the purpose of generating a magnetic field. This term,

    J_d = ε₀ (∂E/∂t)

is the displacement current density. It is not a current in the sense of moving charge — no charge
crosses the gap — but it has the same effect on the magnetic field as if one did. This is the
missing piece that made the four equations self-consistent and, famously, predicted that
electromagnetic waves exist and travel at the speed of light.

### 12.3 Why the loop moves and the flux changes

A common follow-up doubt: if a conducting loop moves through a static magnetic field, does an EMF
appear even though the field itself is not changing in time? Yes — Faraday's law depends on the
*rate of change of flux through the loop*, and flux depends on the loop's area and orientation
relative to the field, not only on the field's own time-dependence. A loop moving into a region of
stronger field, or rotating relative to a fixed field, experiences a changing flux and therefore an
induced EMF, exactly as if the field itself had changed. This is the principle behind electric
generators.

### 12.4 Non-uniform fields

All four equations hold for non-uniform fields; uniformity is a simplification used in worked
examples, not a requirement of the physics. The integral forms of Gauss's and Ampère's laws apply
to any closed surface or loop, however the field varies in space, provided the appropriate flux and
circulation integrals are evaluated correctly.
