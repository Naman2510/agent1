**Dynamic vs. always-accelerator: 2590 vs. 2366 cycles -- dynamic is 224 cycles (9.5%) SLOWER than the naive baseline.**

**Comparison with Phase 15's small 6-workload stream** (read directly from `results/dynamic_scheduling_report.md`, not hand-copied): there, dynamic (492 cycles) was 26.5% worse than always-accelerator (389 cycles). Here, at roughly 4-32x the per-workload scale, dynamic is only 9.5% worse. This confirms the hypothesis Phase 15's finding raised: **the runtime decision's fixed per-block overhead matters proportionally less as each block does more real work** -- the relative penalty shrunk by roughly 3x between the two streams.

**But the ceiling itself is the real limiting factor, not just overhead.** Oracle (2356 cycles) beats always-accelerator (2366 cycles) by only 10 cycles here -- because in this project's real measured data, the accelerator wins essentially every workload except `vecadd` at N=1 (see `docs/scheduler.md`). A perfect, zero-overhead scheduler could only ever have saved 10 cycles on this exact stream, regardless of how good its decisions are or how cheap they are to make. **For this specific accelerator/workload combination, always using the accelerator is already very close to optimal** -- the real crossover this project found (Phase 13's `vecadd N=1`) is a narrow, small-workload corner case, not a broad regime where a learned scheduler has much room to add value. See docs/mixed_workloads.md for the full discussion.
